Welcome,
to Egypt kitchen

This is one of the oldest kitchens in the world. Egyptian kitchens date back to ancient times, where food was cooked using clay ovens and open fires. Traditional ingredients like lentils, onions, and garlic were commonly used in everyday meals.

Main dishes

Mahshi

Mahshi is a popular Egyptian dish made of vegetables like zucchini, eggplant, and bell peppers stuffed with a flavorful mix of rice, herbs, and spices, then cooked in a rich tomato sauce. This beloved comfort food is often served during family gatherings, festive occasions, and traditional feasts. The stuffing typically includes rice mixed with fresh parsley, dill, coriander, onions, garlic, and sometimes minced meat for added richness. Once filled, the vegetables are simmered gently in a savory tomato-based broth until tender and infused with flavor. Mahshi reflects the heart of Egyptian home cooking—warm, nourishing, and made with love.

Side-dishes

Baba Ghanoush

Baba Ghanoush is a creamy, smoky eggplant dip made with tahini, garlic, lemon juice, and olive oil — perfect with pita or fresh veggies. The eggplants are traditionally roasted or grilled until the skin is charred, giving the dip its signature smoky flavor. Once peeled and mashed, they are blended with rich tahini and a touch of tangy lemon juice to create a smooth and flavorful spread. A drizzle of olive oil and a sprinkle of fresh parsley or paprika often top it off. This savory appetizer is not only delicious but also vegan, gluten-free, and packed with nutrients. Whether served as a dip, spread, or side dish, Baba Ghanoush adds a touch of Mediterranean flair to any table.

Desserts

kunafa

Egyptian Kunafa is a traditional dessert that holds a special place in every celebration, especially during Ramadan. Made with fine, shredded dough (kataifi), it’s layered or rolled with creamy fillings like sweetened ricotta, nuts, or even custard. Once baked to a golden crisp, it’s soaked in a fragrant sugar syrup, giving it a deliciously rich and sticky sweetness. What sets Egyptian Kunafa apart is its versatility — from soft and creamy to crunchy and filled, every version is a crowd-pleaser. It’s more than just a dessert — it’s a nostalgic bite of Egyptian culture.

Drinks

Sopia

Sobia is a popular traditional Egyptian drink, especially loved during the holy month of Ramadan. Creamy, sweet, and refreshing, Sobia is made from a blend of coconut milk, rice, sugar, and sometimes a touch of vanilla. It has a smooth, velvety texture and a rich, milky flavor with subtle hints of coconut that make it both comforting and energizing. Served chilled, it’s the perfect beverage to cool down with after a long day of fasting. Often enjoyed at street stalls or prepared fresh at home, Sobia holds a nostalgic place in Egyptian culture, bringing back memories of family gatherings and festive evenings.
